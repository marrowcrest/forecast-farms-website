Hunter, jumper or equitation?

Each ring scores something different, so we train for the ring you want to ride in.

Hunter

The judge scores
The horse: its pace, jumping style and manners, and how evenly it meets each fence.
Suits riders who like
Rhythm, precision and a smooth round.

Jumper

The judge scores
Faults and time. Rails down and refusals cost points, and ties go to a timed jump-off.
Suits riders who like
Tight turns, technical courses and the clock.

Equitation

The judge scores
The rider: position, use of the aids and an accurate track. Some classes add tests.
Suits riders who like
Getting their position right, which helps in every ring.

How Denise teaches

Denise trains horses as well as riders, so she can see where you and your horse each stand.

  • The basics come before height.Fences go up when your position, rhythm and straightness hold.
  • Your horse sets the pace.Its training, fitness and temperament decide how fast you move up.
  • You know what you’re working on.Goals are set at your evaluation and checked at each stage below.
  • She’ll tell you if you’re not ready to show.You go to a show when your rounds at home hold up at that height.

From first lesson to the show ring

Every rider rides the same four stages. You move on when you can do the things listed under your stage.

  1. 1Evaluation

    One lesson with Denise. She watches your position and balance, looks at your horse, and you agree on goals.

    You leave with

    • A recommended program and lesson schedule
    • Your goals for the season

  2. 2Foundation

    An independent seat, rhythm and straightness on the flat, then poles, gymnastics and small fences.

    You move on when you have

    • A secure two-point and a following hand
    • A steady pace through pole exercises
    • Correct leads and balanced transitions

  3. 3Development

    Lines, bending lines and related distances. You count strides and change the canter on purpose.

    You move on when you can

    • Ride the track you planned
    • Lengthen and shorten when asked
    • Find distances without chasing

  4. 4Competition

    Picking divisions, walking courses, warming up, and riding rounds like the ones you ride at home.

    You’re ready when you

    • Walk the course and ride your own plan
    • Ride steady rounds at your height

What riders say

Is this the right barn for you?

We do our best work with riders and owners who ride every week.

We’re a good fit if you

  • Want to ride every week
  • Own or lease a horse, or want to ride one of ours regularly
  • Want to show, or want to get good enough to
  • Care about correct basics and your horse’s soundness
  • Want to be told plainly how you’re doing

We’re probably not if you

  • Want an occasional ride
  • Want trail rides or pony rides
  • Want to move up faster than your horse is ready for
